Transformations of the plane

Stretches, enlargements and rotations as 2×2 matrices, and what the determinant says about area.

Transformations with matrices

A point $(x, y)$ is written as a column, and a figure as the matrix whose columns are its vertices. Multiplying on the left by a $2\times 2$ matrix transforms every point at once:

$$C' = T\cdot C$$

Stretches and enlargement with factor $k$

$$\underbrace{\begin{pmatrix} k & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}}_{\text{horizontal}} \qquad \underbrace{\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & k \end{pmatrix}}_{\text{vertical}} \qquad \underbrace{\begin{pmatrix} k & 0 \\ 0 & k \end{pmatrix}}_{\text{enlargement}}$$

The enlargement with centre $(0,0)$ is both stretches at once.

The figure in the examples

The square with vertices $(1,1)$, $(4,1)$, $(4,4)$ and $(1,4)$, with side $3$ and area $9$:

$$C = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 4 & 4 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 & 4 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$$

Examples

Horizontal stretch with factor 2

$$E_H = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}\qquad C' = E_H\cdot C = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 4 & 4 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 & 4 & 4 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 8 & 8 & 2 \\ 1 & 1 & 4 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$$

$\det(E_H) = 2$. The rectangle is $6\times 3$: area $18$.

Vertical stretch with factor −3

$$E_V = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -3 \end{pmatrix}\qquad C' = E_V\cdot C = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -3 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 4 & 4 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 & 4 & 4 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 4 & 4 & 1 \\ -3 & -3 & -12 & -12 \end{pmatrix}$$

It is a vertical stretch with factor $3$ and a reflection in the $x$-axis. $\det(E_V) = -3$, and the rectangle is $3\times 9$: area $27$.

Enlargement with factor 2

$$H = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}\qquad C' = H\cdot C = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 4 & 4 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 & 4 & 4 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 8 & 8 & 2 \\ 2 & 2 & 8 & 8 \end{pmatrix}$$

$\det(H) = 4$. The image square has side $6$: area $36$.

Correction to the board

On the board the names were crossed: the horizontal stretch was called $EV$ and the vertical one $EH$. Here they are $E_H$ (horizontal) and $E_V$ (vertical). And where it said $|EV| = -3$, the bars are the determinant, not an absolute value: the determinant is $-3$ and the area is multiplied by $|-3| = 3$.

The determinant and area

Relationship

$$\text{Area of the image} = |\det(T)|\times\text{area of the original figure}$$

In the three examples: $18 = 2\cdot 9$, $27 = |-3|\cdot 9$ and $36 = 4\cdot 9$.

The sign of the determinant tells you whether the transformation flips the figure over: with $\det < 0$, as in $E_V$, there is a reflection.

Rotation through an angle θ

Centre at the origin

$$\underbrace{\begin{pmatrix} \cos\theta & -\sin\theta \\ \sin\theta & \cos\theta \end{pmatrix}}_{\text{anticlockwise}} \qquad \underbrace{\begin{pmatrix} \cos\theta & \sin\theta \\ -\sin\theta & \cos\theta \end{pmatrix}}_{\text{clockwise}}$$

Its determinant is $\cos^{2}\theta + \sin^{2}\theta = 1$: a rotation does not change area.

Example: 45° anticlockwise rotation

$$G = \begin{pmatrix} \cos 45^\circ & -\sin 45^\circ \\ \sin 45^\circ & \cos 45^\circ \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} & -\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \\ \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} & \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \end{pmatrix} = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$$
$$C' = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 4 & 4 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 & 4 & 4 \end{pmatrix} = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 3 & 0 & -3 \\ 2 & 5 & 8 & 5 \end{pmatrix} \approx \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 2.12 & 0 & -2.12 \\ 1.41 & 3.54 & 5.66 & 3.54 \end{pmatrix}$$

$|G| = \cos^{2}45^\circ + \sin^{2}45^\circ = 1$: the rotated square still has area $9$.

Correction to the board

On the board it came out as $3.53$, but $\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\cdot 5 = 3.5355\ldots$, which rounds to $3.54$.

The matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ in Powers, properties and the transpose is the $90^\circ$ clockwise rotation: that is why its fourth power is the identity.
