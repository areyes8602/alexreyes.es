Exercise 1

The matrix $P = \begin{pmatrix} 32\,450 \\ 18\,725 \\ 24\,175 \\ 19\,250 \end{pmatrix}$ gives the prices, in dollars, of four car models. Sales tax is $7.5\,\%$ of the purchase price.

a) Write down $T = kP$, where $T$ represents the tax paid on each car.

The tax

$$T = 0.075\,P \approx \begin{pmatrix} 2433.75 \\ 1404.38 \\ 1813.13 \\ 1443.75 \end{pmatrix}$$
b) The matrix $C$ represents the cost of each car including tax. Write a matrix equation giving $C$ in terms of $P$.

The cost including tax

$$C = P + T = P + 0.075\,P$$
$C = 1.075\,P$

In numbers: $C \approx (34\,883.75,\ 20\,129.38,\ 25\,988.13,\ 20\,693.75)$ dollars.

Exercise 2

Using the matrices

$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -2 \\ 2 & 3 \end{pmatrix}, \quad B = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & -4 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix}, \quad C = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ -1 & 2 \\ -2 & 3 \end{pmatrix},$$
$$D = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -2 & -1 \\ 3 & 0 & -4 \\ -3 & 2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \quad E = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \\ -3 \end{pmatrix}, \quad F = \begin{pmatrix} -3 & -1 \\ 2 & 4 \\ 5 & -2 \end{pmatrix},$$

find each of the following matrices if possible. If it is not possible, explain why.

a) $(C + F)A$

$(C + F)A$

$$(C + F)A = \begin{pmatrix} -2 & -1 \\ 1 & 6 \\ 3 & 1 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -2 \\ 2 & 3 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -4 & 1 \\ 13 & 16 \\ 5 & -3 \end{pmatrix}$$
b) $DE$

$DE$

$3\times 3$ times $3\times 1$: it exists and is $3\times 1$.

$$DE = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -2 & -1 \\ 3 & 0 & -4 \\ -3 & 2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \\ -3 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 18 \\ -7 \end{pmatrix}$$

This part was not done in class: I have added it for completeness.

c) $ABC$

$ABC$

$(2\times 2)(2\times 3)(3\times 2)$: it exists and is $2\times 2$.

$$AB = \begin{pmatrix} 4 & -4 & -5 \\ 1 & -8 & 11 \end{pmatrix}\qquad ABC = (AB)\,C = \begin{pmatrix} 18 & -23 \\ -13 & 17 \end{pmatrix}$$

This part was not done in class: I have added it for completeness.

d) $CF$

$CF$

It does not exist: $C$ is $3\times 2$ and $F$ is $3\times 2$, and the $2$ columns of $C$ do not match the $3$ rows of $F$.

This part was not done in class: I have added it for completeness.

e) $2A - 3I_2$

$2A - 3I_2$

$$2\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -2 \\ 2 & 3 \end{pmatrix} - 3\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & -4 \\ 4 & 6 \end{pmatrix} - \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 0 \\ 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -1 & -4 \\ 4 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$$

Exercise 3

A company makes three products, $P_1$, $P_2$ and $P_3$, at each of its four plants, $L_1$, $L_2$, $L_3$ and $L_4$. The matrix $A = \begin{pmatrix} 20 & 35 & 15 \\ 18 & 28 & 12 \\ 45 & 72 & 32 \end{pmatrix}$ gives the daily amount, in kilograms, of carbon monoxide, sulfur dioxide and nitric oxide (by columns) released in making each product (by rows).

New regulations require daily emissions of carbon monoxide, sulfur dioxide and nitric oxide to be cut by $60\,\%$, $20\,\%$ and $40\,\%$ respectively. The company takes just the measures needed to meet these minimum standards.

a) Let $N$ be the matrix whose entries are the total daily kilograms of pollutants released by each product after the measures. Write down the matrix $R$ such that $N = AR$, and find $N$.

The matrix $R$ and the emissions $N$

Cutting by $60\,\%$ leaves $40\,\%$; for the other two, $80\,\%$ and $60\,\%$:

$$N = A\cdot R = \begin{pmatrix} 20 & 35 & 15 \\ 18 & 28 & 12 \\ 45 & 72 & 32 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 0.4 \\ 0.8 \\ 0.6 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 20\cdot 0.4 + 35\cdot 0.8 + 15\cdot 0.6 \\ 18\cdot 0.4 + 28\cdot 0.8 + 12\cdot 0.6 \\ 45\cdot 0.4 + 72\cdot 0.8 + 32\cdot 0.6 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 45 \\ 36.8 \\ 94.8 \end{pmatrix}$$

To get the kilograms of each pollutant separately, $R$ would be the diagonal matrix with $0.4$, $0.8$ and $0.6$, and $N$ would be $3\times 3$.

3×3 system

$$\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 3 \\ 3 & 2 & -1 \\ 2 & 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 30 \\ 20 \\ 10 \end{pmatrix}\qquad |A| = -5$$
$$X = A^{-1}B = \dfrac{1}{5}\begin{pmatrix} -3 & -2 & 7 \\ 5 & 5 & -10 \\ 1 & -1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 30 \\ 20 \\ 10 \end{pmatrix} = \dfrac{1}{5}\begin{pmatrix} -60 \\ 150 \\ 20 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -12 \\ 30 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix}$$
$x = -12, \quad y = 30, \quad z = 4$

Check: $-12 + 30 + 3\cdot 4 = 30$,  $3(-12) + 2\cdot 30 - 4 = 20$  and  $2(-12) + 30 + 4 = 10$.

The daily cost, in dollars, of removing each kilogram of each pollutant (by rows) at each plant (by columns) is given by $C = \begin{pmatrix} 10 & 8 & 12 & 8 \\ 3 & 2 & 4 & 3 \\ 7 & 9 & 6 & 11 \end{pmatrix}$.

b) Find $AC$ and describe the meaning of its entries.

The matrix $AC$

$$AC = \begin{pmatrix} 20 & 35 & 15 \\ 18 & 28 & 12 \\ 45 & 72 & 32 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 10 & 8 & 12 & 8 \\ 3 & 2 & 4 & 3 \\ 7 & 9 & 6 & 11 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 410 & 365 & 470 & 430 \\ 348 & 308 & 400 & 360 \\ 890 & 792 & 1020 & 928 \end{pmatrix}$$

The entry in row $i$ and column $j$ is the daily cost, in dollars, of removing all the pollutants produced by product $P_i$ if it is made at plant $L_j$. For example, $410$: removing the emissions of $P_1$ at $L_1$ costs $410$ dollars a day.

Correction to the board

The wording previously shown here said “the kilograms of each pollutant”; the original asks for the total for each product, which is what $R$ as a column gives, and is how it was solved in class.

2×2 system

$$\begin{pmatrix} 5 & -7 \\ -2 & 5 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 12 \\ 20 \end{pmatrix}\qquad |A| = 25 - 14 = 11$$
$$X = A^{-1}B = \dfrac{1}{11}\begin{pmatrix} 5 & 7 \\ 2 & 5 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 12 \\ 20 \end{pmatrix} = \dfrac{1}{11}\begin{pmatrix} 200 \\ 124 \end{pmatrix}$$
$x = \dfrac{200}{11} \approx 18.18, \qquad y = \dfrac{124}{11} \approx 11.27$

Check: $5\cdot\frac{200}{11} - 7\cdot\frac{124}{11} = \frac{1000 - 868}{11} = \frac{132}{11} = 12$.

Exercise 4

If $A = \begin{pmatrix} -1 & 2 & 4 \\ 3 & 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ and $B = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & -2 \\ -1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, find each of the following if possible. If it is not possible, explain why.

a) $AB$

$AB$

It does not exist: $A$ is $2\times 3$ and $B$ is $2\times 2$, and the $3$ columns of $A$ do not match the $2$ rows of $B$.

b) $BA$

$BA$

$$BA = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & -2 \\ -1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}_{2\times 2}\begin{pmatrix} -1 & 2 & 4 \\ 3 & 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}_{2\times 3} = \begin{pmatrix} -9 & 4 & 12 \\ 4 & -1 & -4 \end{pmatrix}_{2\times 3}$$
c) $A^{2}$

$A^{2}$

It does not exist: $A$ is not square.

d) $B^{2}$

$B^{2}$

$$B^{2} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & -2 \\ -1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 3 & -2 \\ -1 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 11 & -8 \\ -4 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$$

Exercise 5

Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} -3 & 5 \\ 1 & -2 \end{pmatrix}$ and $B = \begin{pmatrix} -2 & -5 \\ -1 & -3 \end{pmatrix}$.

a) Show that $AB = BA = I_2$.

Both products

$$AB = \begin{pmatrix} -3 & 5 \\ 1 & -2 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} -2 & -5 \\ -1 & -3 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = I_2 \qquad BA = \begin{pmatrix} -2 & -5 \\ -1 & -3 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} -3 & 5 \\ 1 & -2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = I_2$$

So $B = A^{-1}$: if $AB = I$ and $A^{-1}$ exists, multiplying on the left gives $A^{-1}AB = A^{-1}I$, that is, $B = A^{-1}$.

b) Find $(2AB)^{10}$.

$(2AB)^{10}$

$$(2AB)^{10} = (2I)^{10} = 2^{10}\,I^{10} = 1024\,I = \begin{pmatrix} 1024 & 0 \\ 0 & 1024 \end{pmatrix}$$

Correction to the board

In class we worked out $\det\big((2AB)^{10}\big) = \left(\det(2I)\right)^{10} = 4^{10} = 2^{20}$, using $\det(AB) = \det A\cdot\det B$ and $\det(A^{n}) = (\det A)^{n}$. That is correct, but the question asks for the matrix $(2AB)^{10}$, not its determinant. The two fit together: $\det(1024\,I) = 1024^{2} = 2^{20}$.
