Measuring angles: radians, arc and sector

What a radian is, how to convert between degrees and radians, and why the arc and sector formulas get much shorter when the angle is measured in radians.

The radian

Definition

A radian is the central angle that subtends an arc whose length equals the radius of the circle.

Since the whole circle measures $2\pi r$, one full turn holds $2\pi$ radii. That gives the two equivalences worth keeping at hand:

$$\pi \ \text{rad} = 180^\circ \qquad\qquad 2\pi \ \text{rad} = 360^\circ$$

The radian has no unit of its own: it is a ratio of lengths (arc over radius), so it is a dimensionless number. That is why you can write $\sin(1.2)$ and it is understood to be in radians.

Converting between radians and degrees

Multiply by the conversion factor, arranged so that the unwanted unit cancels:

$$\dfrac{\pi}{3} \ \cancel{\text{rad}} \cdot \dfrac{180^\circ}{\pi \ \cancel{\text{rad}}} = 60^\circ$$

Example

That is why $\sin(60^\circ)$ and $\sin\!\left(\dfrac{\pi}{3}\right)$ are the same number: the same angle written two ways.

$$\sin(60^\circ) = \sin\!\left(\dfrac{\pi}{3}\right) = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$$

On the calculator

The mode (DEG or RAD) must match the unit the angle is written in. This is the most common mistake in the whole topic: the working is right and the answer is wrong because the calculator was in the other mode.

Arc length and sector area

A sector is the fraction of the circle the angle covers. In degrees that fraction is $\dfrac{\theta}{360}$; in radians, $\dfrac{\theta}{2\pi}$.

In degreesIn radians
Arc length $L = \dfrac{2\pi r\,\theta}{360}$ $L = r\,\theta$
Sector area $A = \dfrac{\pi r^2\,\theta}{360}$ $A = \dfrac{1}{2} r^2 \theta$

Why radians pay off

The formulas on the right come from those on the left by replacing $360^\circ$ with $2\pi$. They are not different formulas: it is the same one, written in the unit that makes the factor vanish. With radians there is no dividing by 360.

Exercise: the goat tied at the corner

The problem

A goat is tied with a $3$ m rope to the corner of a fenced rectangular field measuring $6 \times 2.5$ m. Find the angle $\alpha$ shown in the figure and use it to find the area of grass the goat can reach.

a) The angle α

The rope reaches the top side. The triangle it forms with the left fence is right-angled, and in it the leg adjacent to $\beta$ is $2.5$ and the hypotenuse is $3$:

$$\cos\beta = \dfrac{2{,}5}{3} \;\Longrightarrow\; \beta = \arccos\!\left(\dfrac{2{,}5}{3}\right) = 0{,}586 \ \text{rad}$$

And since the two angles add up to the right angle at the corner:

$$\alpha = \dfrac{\pi}{2} - \beta = 0{,}985 \ \text{rad}$$

b) The area

The region splits in two: the triangle against the left fence and the sector of radius $3$ and angle $\alpha$.

$$A = \underbrace{\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot 2{,}5 \cdot 3 \cdot \sin\beta}_{\text{triangle}} \;+\; \underbrace{\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot 3^2 \cdot \alpha}_{\text{sector}} = 2{,}073 + 4{,}433 = 6{,}51 \ \text{m}^2$$

Careful with rounding halfway through: taking $\alpha = 0.98$ instead of $0.985$ gives $6.48$ m² instead of $6.51$. Carry the full values and round only the final answer.

Exercise: the area shared by two circles

The problem

Two circles of radii $2$ and $3$ have their centres $2$ units apart. Find the area of the region they share.

Join the two centres to one of the intersection points: you get a triangle with sides $2$, $2$ and $3$. Its angles at the centres are the ones that open each sector.

The two angles, by the cosine rule

$$\alpha = \arccos\!\left(\dfrac{2^2+2^2-3^2}{2\cdot 2\cdot 2}\right) = \arccos(-0{,}125) = 1{,}696 \ \text{rad}$$
$$\beta = \arccos\!\left(\dfrac{2^2+3^2-2^2}{2\cdot 2\cdot 3}\right) = \arccos(0{,}75) = 0{,}723 \ \text{rad}$$

The angles $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are those between the line of centres and the radius to an intersection point. Since there are two intersection points, each centre “sees” the region at twice that angle.

Regions II + III · the two sectors of the small circle

$$2 \cdot \dfrac{1}{2}\cdot 2^2 \cdot \alpha = 2 \cdot 3{,}392 = 6{,}784$$

Region I · the segment of the large circle

It is its sector of angle $2\beta$ minus the two triangles left over:

$$\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot 3^2 \cdot (2\beta) \;-\; 2\cdot\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot 2\cdot 3\cdot \sin\beta = 6{,}505 - 3{,}969 = 2{,}536$$

The area we want

$$A = 6{,}784 + 2{,}536 = 9{,}32 \ \text{u}^2$$

Check: the closed formula for the lens area gives $9.3205$ u². If $\beta$ is taken as $0.7317$ instead of $0.7227$, the total comes out $9.36$ and no longer matches.

The idea that keeps coming back

A piece of a circle bounded by a chord (a circular segment) is always sector minus triangle. And the triangle needs $\frac{1}{2}ab\sin C$, which needs the angle: that is why the cosine rule comes first.
