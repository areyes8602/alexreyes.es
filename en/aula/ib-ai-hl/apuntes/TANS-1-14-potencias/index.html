Powers, properties and the transpose

Powers of a square matrix, which properties matrix multiplication has (and which it lacks), and the transpose.

Powers of a matrix

Definition

Only square matrices have powers (otherwise $A\cdot A$ cannot be worked out):

$$A^{n} = \underbrace{A\cdot A\cdots A}_{n\ \text{times}}$$

Example

$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$$
$$A^{2} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}\qquad A^{3} = A^{2}\cdot A = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$$
$$A^{4} = A^{3}\cdot A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = I_2 \qquad A^{5} = A^{4}\cdot A = I_2\cdot A = A$$

The powers repeat every four, so for a very high power it is enough to divide the exponent by $4$ and keep the remainder:

$$A^{2026} = A^{4\cdot 506 + 2} = \left(A^{4}\right)^{506}\cdot A^{2} = I^{506}\cdot A^{2} = A^{2} = \begin{pmatrix} -1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$$

Correction to the board

In class we wrote $2026 = 4\cdot 505 + 2$, but $4\cdot 505 + 2 = 2022$. The right split is $2026 = 4\cdot 506 + 2$. The answer does not change, because what matters is the remainder, $2$.

It is the same cycle as the powers of $i$: this $A$ is the $90^\circ$ clockwise rotation (see Transformations of the plane), and four turns make a full circle. The second example from class, with Fibonacci, is in Powers of a matrix and Fibonacci.

Properties of matrix multiplication

With $A, E \in \mathcal{M}_{n\times n}$,  $B, C \in \mathcal{M}_{n\times m}$ where $n \neq m$,  and $D \in \mathcal{M}_{m\times n}$:

Matrix multiplication is not commutative

Even when both products exist, the order matters. For example:

$$\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}\qquad \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$$

They may coincide in particular cases (with $E = I$, or with $E = A$), but you cannot rely on it.

The transpose

Definition

The transpose of $A$ is written $A^{t}$ (or $A^{T}$) and comes from swapping rows and columns: $\left(A^{t}\right)_{ij} = a_{ji}$. If $A$ is $n\times m$, $A^{t}$ is $m\times n$.

Example

$$A_{3\times 2} = \begin{pmatrix} \textcolor{#16a34a}{1} & \textcolor{#16a34a}{2} \\ \textcolor{#ca8a04}{3} & \textcolor{#ca8a04}{0} \\ \textcolor{#db2777}{-1} & \textcolor{#db2777}{2} \end{pmatrix} \qquad A^{t}_{2\times 3} = \begin{pmatrix} \textcolor{#16a34a}{1} & \textcolor{#ca8a04}{3} & \textcolor{#db2777}{-1} \\ \textcolor{#16a34a}{2} & \textcolor{#ca8a04}{0} & \textcolor{#db2777}{2} \end{pmatrix}$$
