Challenge 1: the Àgora mural

A mural for the neighbourhood day centre: edges, scale, areas, paint cans and percentages. With solutions.

The problem

Today my team has been given a new challenge we are really excited about: the neighbourhood community association has asked us whether we can paint a mural on the wall of the new day centre for the elderly. The coordinator has sent us the design and asked us how much paint of each colour we need. The budget is tight, so she wants us to be as accurate as possible.

To be honest, we need help.

Notes

  1. The planned scale is that $1$ cm on the drawing is $75$ cm in real life.
  2. The real wall is $6$ m long and $5$ m high.
  3. The drawing must be perfectly centred.
  4. Each spray can holds $200$ ml of paint and covers about $1{,}5\ \text{m}^2$. Each purple can costs €$4{,}58$, each pink can €$4{,}00$, each light blue can €$4{,}25$ and each dark blue can €$4{,}40$.
  5. The letters do not count: we already have black paint.

Guidelines. We want complete calculations, using the appropriate combined operations. Any decimals are rounded to two decimal places. It matters that what we are doing can be understood.

Our plan

All this looks complicated, so we have made an outline of the steps we will follow. We will need to revise Pythagoras' theorem, similarity, some areas and perhaps percentages.

  1. Work out every edge missing from the design. The design measurements are not real ones: we need to calculate.
  2. Convert the drawing measurements into real ones and sketch it.
  3. Work out the areas to know the square metres of each colour.
  4. Work out exactly where the design goes so that it is centred on the wall.
  5. With the square metres, order exactly the paint cans we need, using combined operations that can be followed: when money is involved, the coordinator wants it clear.
  6. What percentage of the whole wall does each colour cover?
  7. Which maths concepts did we use in each question?

1. The missing edges

Edge 1

It is the side of the left square, which is given. It is also the diameter of both semicircles and the height of the right square.

Edge 1: $2$ cm

Edge 3

The top vertex of the dark blue triangle is at the midpoint of the top side of the square, which splits it in two halves:

$$\dfrac{2}{2} = 1 \text{ cm}$$

Edge 3: $1$ cm

Edge 2

It is the hypotenuse of a right triangle with legs $2$ cm (the height of the square) and $1$ cm (edge 3). Pythagoras:

$$x^2 = 2^2 + 1^2 = 5 \;\Longrightarrow\; x = \sqrt{5} \approx 2{,}24 \text{ cm}$$

Edge 2: $2{,}24$ cm

Edge 4

It is the diagonal of the right-hand rectangle, the hypotenuse of a right triangle with legs $1{,}5$ cm and $2$ cm:

$$y^2 = 1{,}5^2 + 2^2 = 2{,}25 + 4 = 6{,}25 \;\Longrightarrow\; y = \sqrt{6{,}25} = 2{,}5 \text{ cm}$$

Edge 4: $2{,}5$ cm

Edge 5

It is the width of the right-hand column, which is given.

Edge 5: $1{,}5$ cm

Edge 6

The right-hand column is $4{,}5$ cm high and the rectangle underneath is $2$ cm:

$$4{,}5 - 2 = 2{,}5 \text{ cm}$$

Edge 6: $2{,}5$ cm

3. The square metres of each colour

Pink

In the left square, the two pink corners add up to half of the $1{,}5$ m square. In the right-hand rectangle there is a right triangle with legs $1{,}5$ m and $1{,}13$ m.

$$1{,}5 \cdot 0{,}75 + \dfrac{1{,}5 \cdot 1{,}13}{2} \approx 1{,}97 \text{ m}^2$$
$$1{,}97 \text{ m}^2$$
Purple

A semicircle of radius $0{,}75$ m and the top rectangle, $1{,}13$ m by $1{,}88$ m.

$$\dfrac{\pi \cdot 0{,}75^2}{2} + 1{,}13 \cdot 1{,}88 \approx 3{,}01 \text{ m}^2$$
$$3{,}01 \text{ m}^2$$
Dark blue

The triangle in the left square: base $1{,}5$ m and height $1{,}5$ m.

$$\dfrac{1{,}5 \cdot 1{,}5}{2} \approx 1{,}13 \text{ m}^2$$
$$1{,}13 \text{ m}^2$$
Light blue

The other semicircle, radius $0{,}75$ m, and the triangle below the diagonal, legs $1{,}5$ m and $1{,}13$ m.

$$\dfrac{\pi \cdot 0{,}75^2}{2} + \dfrac{1{,}5 \cdot 1{,}13}{2} \approx 1{,}73 \text{ m}^2$$
$$1{,}73 \text{ m}^2$$

4. Centring the mural on the wall

Horizontal margin

The drawing is $2 + 2 + 1{,}5 = 5{,}5$ cm wide. Using the scale as a conversion factor:

$$5{,}5 \text{ cm} \cdot \dfrac{75 \text{ cm}}{1 \text{ cm}} = 412{,}5 \text{ cm} \approx 4{,}13 \text{ m}$$

The wall is $6$ m long: what is left over is shared between both sides.

$$\dfrac{6 - 4{,}13}{2} \approx 0{,}94 \text{ m}$$

$0{,}94$ m margin on the left and on the right

Vertical margin

The drawing is $4{,}5$ cm high:

$$4{,}5 \text{ cm} \cdot \dfrac{75 \text{ cm}}{1 \text{ cm}} = 337{,}5 \text{ cm} \approx 3{,}38 \text{ m}$$
$$\dfrac{5 - 3{,}38}{2} = 0{,}81 \text{ m}$$

$0{,}81$ m margin at the top and at the bottom

5. The paint cans

Each can covers $1{,}5\ \text{m}^2$. We use this as a conversion factor and always round up: you cannot buy part of a can, and if we run short the mural is left unfinished.

Pink
$$1{,}97 \text{ m}^2 \cdot \dfrac{1 \text{ can}}{1{,}5 \text{ m}^2} \approx 1{,}31 \text{ cans}$$

2 cans

Purple
$$3{,}01 \text{ m}^2 \cdot \dfrac{1 \text{ can}}{1{,}5 \text{ m}^2} \approx 2{,}01 \text{ cans}$$

3 cans

Dark blue
$$1{,}13 \text{ m}^2 \cdot \dfrac{1 \text{ can}}{1{,}5 \text{ m}^2} \approx 0{,}75 \text{ cans}$$

1 can

Light blue
$$1{,}73 \text{ m}^2 \cdot \dfrac{1 \text{ can}}{1{,}5 \text{ m}^2} \approx 1{,}15 \text{ cans}$$

2 cans

Purple shows why we round up: $2{,}01$ cans means two are not enough, however small the shortfall.

Total cost

With the prices in note 4, in a single combined operation:

$$2 \cdot 4{,}00 + 3 \cdot 4{,}58 + 1 \cdot 4{,}40 + 2 \cdot 4{,}25 = 8{,}00 + 13{,}74 + 4{,}40 + 8{,}50 = 34{,}64 \text{ euros}$$

€$34{,}64$ on paint
