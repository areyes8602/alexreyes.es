Challenge 4: is 0.999… equal to 1?

My classmate says 0.9 recurring is equal to 1. Is he right? A figure to see it and the solution hidden underneath.

Download the sheet for challenges 3 and 4 (PDF, in Catalan)

The problem

Wow, my classmate told me that $0{,}\overline{9}$ is equal to $1$. What do you think?

$0{,}\overline{9} = 0{,}9999\ldots$, with infinitely many nines.

See it

Each row is the piece still missing to reach $1$, split into $10$ parts. Each nine we add fills $9$ of them and leaves one empty, which is the one split up in the row below. Add nines and see how much is missing.

0,9

With any number of nines, however large, something is still missing. But $0{,}\overline{9}$ does not have a number of nines: it has infinitely many. So what happens to the missing piece?

What do you think?

Is it true that $0{,}\overline{9} = 1$? Justify your answer.

The generating fraction

In the numerator we write the number without the decimal point up to the end of the first repeating block ($09 = 9$) and subtract the part that does not repeat ($0$). In the denominator, as many nines as there are digits in the repeating block: one.

$$0{,}\overline{9} = \dfrac{9 - 0}{9} = \dfrac{9}{9} = 1$$

$0{,}\overline{9} = 1$

Another way: multiply by 10

This is where the generating-fraction rule comes from. Let $N = 0{,}999\ldots$ and multiply it by $10$: there are still infinitely many nines after the decimal point.

$$10N = 9{,}999\ldots$$
$$10N - N = 9{,}999\ldots - 0{,}999\ldots = 9$$
$$9N = 9 \;\Longrightarrow\; N = 1$$

$N = 1$

And a third: thirds

We know that $\dfrac{1}{3} = 0{,}333\ldots$ Multiplying everything by $3$:

$$3 \cdot \dfrac{1}{3} = 3 \cdot 0{,}333\ldots \;\Longrightarrow\; 1 = 0{,}999\ldots$$

Two ways of writing the same number.

Why is it hard to believe? Because with a finite number of nines something is always missing (you saw it in the figure). With infinitely many nines, what is missing is smaller than any positive number you can think of, and the only number like that is $0$. There is no number between $0{,}\overline{9}$ and $1$: they are the same number written in two ways.
