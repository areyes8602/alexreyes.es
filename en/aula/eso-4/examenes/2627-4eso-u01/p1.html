Question 1 · Making tin cans

A cylinder with given volume and radius: the height, the tin needed, the cost and the budget.

Maximum score · 5 points

Sara wants to start a tin can manufacturing company. Each can must have the shape in the figure: a volume of $141.4\ \text{cm}^3$ and a radius of $3$ cm.

To make the cans she must use sheets of tin. She has contacted a tin factory that sells it at €$4.45$ per $\text{m}^2$. Sara has to make $5000$ cans every month and has a monthly budget of €$400$ to buy the tin sheets she needs.

  1. How many square metres does Sara need to produce each can? 2 p
  2. How many € will it cost to make each can? (If you could not find the previous area, you may use this data: each can has an area of $175\ \text{cm}^2$.) 1.5 p
  3. Will she be able to buy all the tin she needs to make all the cans with her budget, or will she need a loan? (If you could not find the previous area, you may use this data: making each can costs €$0.05$.) 1.5 p

Step-by-step solution

a)

First we find the height from the cylinder volume: $V = \pi r^2 h$.

$$141{,}4 = \pi \cdot 3^2 \cdot h \;\Longrightarrow\; h = \dfrac{141{,}4}{9\pi} \approx 5 \text{ cm}$$

The tin needed is the total area of the cylinder, $A_T = 2A_B + A_L$:

$$A_T = 2\pi r^2 + 2\pi r h = 2\pi \cdot 3^2 + 2\pi \cdot 3 \cdot 5 = 18\pi + 30\pi = 48\pi \approx 150{,}8 \text{ cm}^2$$

Since the price is per $\text{m}^2$, we convert to square metres ($1\ \text{m}^2 = 10\,000\ \text{cm}^2$):

$$150{,}8\ \text{cm}^2 \cdot \dfrac{1\ \text{m}^2}{10\,000\ \text{cm}^2} \approx 0{,}0151\ \text{m}^2$$
Result: Each can needs $\approx 0.0151\ \text{m}^2$ of tin.

b)

We multiply the area in $\text{m}^2$ by the price of tin:

$$0{,}0151 \cdot 4{,}45 \approx 0{,}067 \text{ €}$$

With the alternative data: $0.0175 \cdot 4.45 \approx 0.078$ €.

Result: Each can costs $\approx 0.067$ € in tin.

c)

Monthly cost of the $5000$ cans:

$$5000 \cdot 0{,}067 \approx 335{,}5 \text{ €} \le 400 \text{ €}$$

With the alternative data: $5000 \cdot 0.05 = 250$ €, not needed either.

Result: The budget is enough ($\approx 335.5$ € $< 400$ €): no loan is needed.
