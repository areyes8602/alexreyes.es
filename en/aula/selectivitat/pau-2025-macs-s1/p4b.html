Exercise 4 · Matrices and maximum revenue (Option B)

Matrix product to compare budgets and optimisation of a quadratic revenue function.

Maximum score · 2.5 points

The Catalan Congress on Mathematics Education (C2EM) will be attended by 1,000 people: 10% university teachers, 50% secondary-school teachers and 40% early-childhood and primary teachers. The organisers want to give each of them a gift of type $D_1$, $D_2$ or $D_3$ respectively. They have requested quotes from three companies ($E_1$, $E_2$, $E_3$). The matrix of unit prices (in €) is:

$P = \begin{pmatrix} 1{,}25 & 1 & 1{,}25 \\ 0{,}75 & 1 & 1{,}15 \\ 1 & 0{,}85 & 0{,}80 \end{pmatrix}$   (rows: $D_1$, $D_2$, $D_3$; columns: $E_1$, $E_2$, $E_3$).

  1. Using a matrix product, find which company offers the best price and what that price is. 1.25 p
  2. A hotel near the Congress venue offers double rooms at 80 €/night and estimates 100 bookings. Each euro of discount generates 2 more bookings. Let $x$ be the number of euros of discount applied. Write the function giving the hotel's revenue as a function of $x$. What should the price be to maximise revenue? 1.25 p
Bachillerato CCSS · Block D — Algebraic sense Matrices Optimisation

Step-by-step solution

Key idea: the order vector is $\mathbf{c} = (100,\, 500,\, 400)$ (quantities of $D_1$, $D_2$, $D_3$ for the 1,000 attendees). The cost for each company is the product $\mathbf{c} \cdot P$, which gives a row vector whose components are the total cost with that company.

a) Best company via matrix product

The order: 10% of 1,000 = 100 gifts $D_1$, 50% = 500 gifts $D_2$, 40% = 400 gifts $D_3$. Row vector of the order:

$$\mathbf{c} = (100,\ 500,\ 400).$$

We compute $\mathbf{c} \cdot P$:

$$\mathbf{c} \cdot P = (100,\ 500,\ 400) \begin{pmatrix} 1{,}25 & 1 & 1{,}25 \\ 0{,}75 & 1 & 1{,}15 \\ 1 & 0{,}85 & 0{,}80 \end{pmatrix}.$$

Component 1 ($E_1$): $100 \times 1{,}25 + 500 \times 0{,}75 + 400 \times 1 = 125 + 375 + 400 = \mathbf{900}$ €.

Component 2 ($E_2$): $100 \times 1 + 500 \times 1 + 400 \times 0{,}85 = 100 + 500 + 340 = \mathbf{940}$ €.

Component 3 ($E_3$): $100 \times 1{,}25 + 500 \times 1{,}15 + 400 \times 0{,}80 = 125 + 575 + 320 = \mathbf{1020}$ €.

$$\mathbf{c} \cdot P = (900,\ 940,\ 1020).$$
Company $E_1$ offers the best price: 900 €.

b) Revenue function and optimal price

If $x$ euros of discount are applied:

Room price: $80 - x$ euros.

Number of bookings: $100 + 2x$.

Revenue function:

$$I(x) = (80 - x)(100 + 2x) = 8000 + 160x - 100x - 2x^{2} = -2x^{2} + 60x + 8000.$$

This is a downward-opening parabola ($a = -2 < 0$), so its vertex is the maximum. We differentiate:

$$I'(x) = -4x + 60 = 0 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad x = 15.$$

$I''(x) = -4 < 0$ ✓ (maximum).

The optimal discount is 15 €, so the final price is $80 - 15 = \mathbf{65}$ €/night.

Maximum revenue: $I(15) = (80-15)(100+30) = 65 \times 130 = \mathbf{8450}$ €.

The price that maximises revenue is 65 €/night, with a maximum revenue of 8,450 €.
