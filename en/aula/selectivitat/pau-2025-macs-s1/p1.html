Exercise 1 · Systems of equations — Hotel rooms

System with three unknowns, expressing the solutions and comparing costs.

Maximum score · 2.5 points

We want to accommodate 10 people in a hotel that has single, double and triple rooms. We know that if we book six single rooms and two double rooms, we must pay 702 €, and that if we book one single room and three triple rooms, we pay the same as if we book two double rooms and two triple rooms.

  1. Express the prices of the double room and the triple room as a function of the single-room price. 1.25 p
  2. If the triple-room price is twice the single-room price, what is the price of each type of room? Of the three options proposed to accommodate the 10 people, which gives the lowest price and what is that price? 1.25 p
Bachillerato CCSS · Block D — Algebraic sense Linear systems Modelling

Step-by-step solution

Key idea: let $i$, $d$ and $t$ be the prices of the single, double and triple rooms (in €). The conditions in the statement give two equations with three unknowns; hence we express $d$ and $t$ as a function of $i$. With the extra condition in part b) the system becomes determined.

a) Prices as a function of the single room

The first condition: 6 single rooms + 2 double rooms cost 702 €.

$$6i + 2d = 702 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad d = \dfrac{702 - 6i}{2} = 351 - 3i.$$

The second condition: 1 single + 3 triples = 2 doubles + 2 triples.

$$i + 3t = 2d + 2t \quad \Longrightarrow \quad i + t = 2d \quad \Longrightarrow \quad t = 2d - i.$$

Substituting $d = 351 - 3i$:

$$t = 2(351 - 3i) - i = 702 - 6i - i = 702 - 7i.$$
$d = 351 - 3i$  and  $t = 702 - 7i$

b) Specific prices and cheapest option

Extra condition: $t = 2i$.

$$702 - 7i = 2i \quad \Longrightarrow \quad 702 = 9i \quad \Longrightarrow \quad i = 78\ \text{€}.$$

We obtain the three prices:

$$d = 351 - 3(78) = 351 - 234 = 117\ \text{€}, \qquad t = 2(78) = 156\ \text{€}.$$

Check: $6(78) + 2(117) = 468 + 234 = 702$ ✓  |  $78 + 3(156) = 78 + 468 = 546$ and $2(117) + 2(156) = 234 + 312 = 546$ ✓

The three options to accommodate the 10 people (each adds up to 10 people):

Option 1 — 6 singles + 2 doubles (6·1 + 2·2 = 10): $6(78) + 2(117) = 468 + 234 = \mathbf{702}$ €.

Option 2 — 1 single + 3 triples (1 + 3·3 = 10): $1(78) + 3(156) = 78 + 468 = \mathbf{546}$ €.

Option 3 — 2 doubles + 2 triples (2·2 + 2·3 = 10): $2(117) + 2(156) = 234 + 312 = \mathbf{546}$ €.

Options 2 and 3 tie at the lowest price: 546 €.
