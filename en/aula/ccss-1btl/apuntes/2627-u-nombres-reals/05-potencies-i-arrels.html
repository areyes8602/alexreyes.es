5. Powers and roots

The properties of powers and of radicals, with the sign and index traps.

Powers

Definition

If $a$ is a real number, called the base, and $n$ is an integer, called the exponent, we define

$$a^{n} = \underbrace{a \cdot a \cdot a \cdots a}_{n \text{ times}}$$

with $a \in \mathbb{R}$ and $n \in \mathbb{Z}$.

PropertyExpressionExample
Product of powers with the same base$a^{n} \cdot a^{m} = a^{n+m}$$(-2)^{2} \cdot (-2)^{3} = (-2)^{5} = -32$
Quotient of powers with the same base$\dfrac{a^{n}}{a^{m}} = a^{n-m}$$\dfrac{2^{8}}{2^{3}} = 2^{5} = 32$
Power of a power$(a^{n})^{m} = a^{n \cdot m}$$\left[\left(\tfrac{2}{3}\right)^{5}\right]^{2} = \left(\tfrac{2}{3}\right)^{10}$
Power of a product$(a \cdot b)^{n} = a^{n} \cdot b^{n}$$(2x)^{3} = 2^{3} x^{3} = 8x^{3}$
Power of a quotient$\left(\dfrac{a}{b}\right)^{n} = \dfrac{a^{n}}{b^{n}}$$\left(\dfrac{x}{2y}\right)^{5} = \dfrac{x^{5}}{2^{5} y^{5}}$
Negative exponent$a^{-n} = \dfrac{1}{a^{n}}$$(-2)^{-3} = \dfrac{1}{(-2)^{3}} = -\dfrac{1}{8}$
Fractional exponent$a^{p/q} = \sqrt[q]{a^{p}}$$3^{5/2} = \sqrt{3^{5}}$
Zero exponent$a^{0} = 1$$(-2xc)^{0} = 1$
$(-a)^{n} \neq -a^{n}$$(-a)^{n} = \begin{cases} a^{n} & \text{if } n \text{ is even} \\ -a^{n} & \text{if } n \text{ is odd}\end{cases}$$(-2)^{2} = 4$
$(-2)^{3} = -8$

The one everybody trips on

The minus sign only goes into the power when it sits inside the brackets:

$$-2^{2} = -(2 \cdot 2) = -4 \qquad (-2)^{2} = (-2) \cdot (-2) = +2^{2} = 4$$
$$(-2)^{3} = (-2)(-2)(-2) = -2^{3} = -8$$

Negative exponents and fractions

A negative exponent flips the fraction:

$$\left(\dfrac{a}{b}\right)^{-n} = \left(\dfrac{b}{a}\right)^{n}$$

A whole number works the same way, because $5 = \dfrac{5}{1}$:

$$5^{-3} = \left(\dfrac{5}{1}\right)^{-3} = \left(\dfrac{1}{5}\right)^{3} = \dfrac{1^{3}}{5^{3}} = \dfrac{1}{5^{3}}$$
$$\left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)^{-5} = \left(\dfrac{3}{2}\right)^{5}$$

Radicals

Definition

The $n$-th root of $A$ is the number $B$ such that $B^{n} = A$:

$$\sqrt[n]{A} = B \iff B^{n} = A$$

$A$ is the radicand, $n$ is the index of the root, and the whole expression $\sqrt[n]{A}$ is called a radical.

In the table below, $a$ and $b$ are positive. Without that condition the product and quotient rules stop holding.

PropertyExpressionExample
Product of roots with the same index$\sqrt[n]{a} \cdot \sqrt[n]{b} = \sqrt[n]{a \cdot b}$$\sqrt[3]{3} \cdot \sqrt[3]{9} = \sqrt[3]{27} = 3$
Quotient of roots with the same index$\dfrac{\sqrt[n]{a}}{\sqrt[n]{b}} = \sqrt[n]{\dfrac{a}{b}}$$\dfrac{\sqrt[3]{16}}{\sqrt[3]{2}} = \sqrt[3]{8} = 2$
Power of a root$\left(\sqrt[n]{a}\right)^{p} = \sqrt[n]{a^{p}}$$\left(\sqrt[3]{x}\right)^{2} = \sqrt[3]{x^{2}}$
Root of a root$\sqrt[n]{\sqrt[m]{a}} = \sqrt[n \cdot m]{a}$$\sqrt[3]{\sqrt{x}} = \sqrt[6]{x}$
Root of a product$\sqrt[n]{a \cdot b} = \sqrt[n]{a} \cdot \sqrt[n]{b}$$\sqrt[3]{x^{2} y} = \sqrt[3]{x^{2}} \cdot \sqrt[3]{y}$
Root of a quotient$\sqrt[n]{\dfrac{a}{b}} = \dfrac{\sqrt[n]{a}}{\sqrt[n]{b}}$$\sqrt[3]{\dfrac{xb}{z}} = \dfrac{\sqrt[3]{x} \cdot \sqrt[3]{b}}{\sqrt[3]{z}}$
Simplifying the radicand
with $p = n \cdot c + r$		$\sqrt[n]{a^{p}} = a^{c} \cdot \sqrt[n]{a^{r}}$$\sqrt[3]{x^{14}} = \sqrt[3]{x^{12+2}} = x^{4} \cdot \sqrt[3]{x^{2}}$
Simplifying the index$\sqrt[k \cdot n]{a^{k \cdot p}} = \sqrt[n]{a^{p}}$$\sqrt[12]{b^{8}} = \sqrt[3]{b^{2}}$
Taking factors into the radicand$a^{p} \cdot \sqrt[n]{b} = \sqrt[n]{b \cdot a^{n \cdot p}}$$m^{7} \cdot \sqrt[4]{m^{3}} = \sqrt[4]{(m^{7})^{4} \cdot m^{3}} = \sqrt[4]{m^{31}}$
Changing the index$\sqrt[n]{a^{p}} = \sqrt[n \cdot k]{a^{p \cdot k}}$$\sqrt[3]{x} = \sqrt[30]{x^{10}}$
Sum of roots with the same index$\sqrt[n]{a} + \sqrt[n]{b} \neq \sqrt[n]{a + b}$$\sqrt[3]{1} + \sqrt[3]{1} \neq \sqrt[3]{2}$

Why they have to be positive

The product rule comes from the fractional exponent:

$$a^{1/n} \cdot b^{1/n} = (a \cdot b)^{1/n} = \sqrt[n]{ab}$$

Applied carelessly to negatives with an even index it produces nonsense: $\sqrt{6}$ is not $\sqrt{-2} \cdot \sqrt{-3}$, because $\sqrt{-2}$ and $\sqrt{-3}$ do not exist in $\mathbb{R}$.

Examples

$$-5^{2/3} = -\sqrt[3]{5^{2}}$$
$$\sqrt[5]{-2^{4}} = \left(-2^{4}\right)^{1/5} = -2^{4/5}$$

With an odd index the root of a negative number does exist, and the minus sign comes out in front.

Left open in class

These two were not finished:

$$\sqrt[3]{5} \cdot \sqrt[4]{7} \qquad\qquad 5^{2/3} \cdot 3^{2/3}$$

The first one calls for changing the index (the indices differ); the second one, for the power of a product (the exponents do match).
