3. Intervals

What an interval is, how it is written, and what the union and the intersection of two intervals are.

What an interval is

Definition

An interval represents a set of real numbers lying between two given real values.

Types

→ Open $(\;,\;)$: the endpoints are not included.

→ Half-open $(\;,\;]$ and $[\;,\;)$: one endpoint is included.

→ Closed $[\;,\;]$: both endpoints are included.

A square bracket means "this endpoint is in" and a round bracket, "this endpoint is out". On the line we draw a solid dot or a hollow one.

Notation

IntervalSet notationReal line
$(a,\, b)$ $\{a < x < b \mid x \in \mathbb{R}\}$
$[a,\, b)$ $\{a \le x < b \mid x \in \mathbb{R}\}$
$(a,\, b]$ $\{a < x \le b \mid x \in \mathbb{R}\}$
$[a,\, b]$ $\{a \le x \le b \mid x \in \mathbb{R}\}$

Example

IntervalSet notationReal line
$[0,\, 5)$ $\{0 \le x < 5 \mid x \in \mathbb{R}\}$

The $0$ is in and the $5$ is out: $4{,}999\ldots$ belongs to it, $5$ does not.

Union and intersection

Union $\cup$

The union of two intervals $A$ and $B$ is made of the values that are in $A$ or in $B$.

Example

$$(-3,\, 5] \;\cup\; (-1,\, 7) = (-3,\, 7)$$

The two intervals overlap, so the union is a single interval: it starts where the first one starts and ends where the second one ends.

Intersection $\cap$

The intersection of two intervals $A$ and $B$ is made of the values that are in $A$ and in $B$ at the same time.

Example

$$(-3,\, 5] \;\cap\; (-1,\, 7) = (-1,\, 5]$$

The intersection is the stretch they share: it starts at the later start and ends at the earlier end. Each endpoint keeps the bracket it had in its own interval.
