Number sets

Natural numbers, integers and rational numbers: what they are for, how they are written and how to place a fraction on the number line.

Natural numbers

Used for counting.

$$\mathbb{N} = \{1,\ 2,\ 3,\ 4,\ \ldots\}$$

Integers

Used for counting forwards and backwards.

$$\mathbb{Z} = \{\ldots,\ -4,\ -3,\ -2,\ -1,\ 0,\ 1,\ 2,\ 3,\ 4,\ \ldots\}$$

Rational numbers

Used, among other things, for sharing out. They are all the numbers that can be written as a fraction.

$$\mathbb{Q} = \left\{ \dfrac{a}{b} \;\middle|\; a, b \in \mathbb{Z},\ b \neq 0 \right\}$$

Read it as: “the set of fractions $\frac{a}{b}$ where $a$ and $b$ are integers and $b$ is not $0$”.

Examples

$\dfrac{3}{4}$✓ is a fraction
$\dfrac{5{,}2}{3}$✗ is not a fraction: $5{,}2$ is not an integer.
$\dfrac{0}{3} = 0$✓ is a fraction
$\dfrac{-5}{0}$✗ is not a fraction: the denominator cannot be $0$: you cannot share something into zero parts.

Parts of a fraction

$$\dfrac{\textcolor{#0891b2}{a}}{\textcolor{#db2777}{b}}$$
→ Numerator ($a$): the parts we take. → Denominator ($b$): the parts each unit is divided into.

Fractions on the number line

Method

→ The denominator tells you how many equal parts each unit is divided into.

→ The numerator tells you how many of those parts to count from $0$: to the right if it is positive, to the left if it is negative.

Example. To place $\dfrac{7}{3}$, divide each unit into $3$ parts and count $7$ of them from $0$: the point lands between $2$ and $3$.

Try it: change the numerator and the denominator and see where the fraction lands.

Rational decimals

→ Terminating decimal: it has a finite number of decimal digits. $5{,}2$

→ Purely recurring decimal: a digit or group of digits (the period) repeats for ever straight after the decimal comma. $5{,}\overline{2} = 5{,}222\ldots$

→ Mixed recurring decimal: before the period there are digits that do not repeat. $5{,}0\overline{2} = 5{,}0222\ldots$

All three can be written as a fraction, which is why they are rational.
