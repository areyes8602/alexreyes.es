1. Initial definitions

What a fraction is, when it is not one, and what its parts are.

Definition of a fraction

Definition

A fraction is a quotient of 2 integers. The denominator cannot be 0.

$$\mathbb{Q} = \left\{\,\dfrac{a}{b} \;:\; a,\,b \in \mathbb{Z},\; b \neq 0 \,\right\}$$

Example

$\dfrac{3}{5}$ is a fraction
$\dfrac{2.5}{7}$ is not a fraction The numerator $2.5$ is not an integer.
$\dfrac{0}{5}$ is a fraction
$\dfrac{5}{0}$ is not a fraction The denominator is $0$, and you cannot divide by $0$.

Example 2

$\dfrac{3}{4}$
$\dfrac{6}{8}$

Notice that both fractions have the same shaded portion.

Parts of a fraction

$$\dfrac{\textcolor{#06b6d4}{a}}{\textcolor{#ec4899}{b}}$$
→ Numerator ($a$): the parts taken. → Denominator ($b$): the parts each unit is divided into.

Types of fractions

Definition

By comparing the numerator with the denominator we distinguish three types of fraction:

→ Proper, if $N < D$. It is worth less than one unit.

→ Equal to one, if $N = D$. It is worth exactly $1$.

→ Improper, if $N > D$. It is worth more than one unit, so we need more than one unit to represent it.

$\dfrac{2}{5}$ Proper: $2 < 5$, it does not fill the unit.
$\dfrac{3}{3}$ Equal to one: $3 = 3$, it fills the unit exactly.
$\dfrac{5}{3}$ Improper: $5 > 3$, two units are needed to represent it.

Notice that the unit is always the same size. What changes is how many pieces we cut it into (the denominator) and how many we shade (the numerator).

Careful!

With negative fractions, the comparison is made in absolute value. For example, $-\dfrac{5}{3}$ is also improper, because $5 > 3$; its value is less than $-1$.

Mixed numbers

Definition

Every improper fraction can be written as an integer plus a proper fraction. Written this way, it is called a mixed number.

To find it, we carry out the integer division of the numerator by the denominator:

  • the quotient is the integer part,
  • the remainder is the new numerator,
  • the denominator does not change.

Example. Let us write $\dfrac{7}{3}$ as a mixed number. Dividing $7$ by $3$: the quotient is $2$ and the remainder is $1$.

$$7 = 3 \cdot 2 + 1 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \dfrac{7}{3} = \dfrac{3 \cdot 2 + 1}{3} = 2 + \dfrac{1}{3}$$
To go back the other way, multiply the integer part by the denominator and add the numerator: $2 + \dfrac{1}{3} = \dfrac{2 \cdot 3 + 1}{3} = \dfrac{7}{3}$.

Example

$\dfrac{7}{3}$ Improper: three units are needed. As a mixed number, $\dfrac{7}{3} = 2 + \dfrac{1}{3}$.
